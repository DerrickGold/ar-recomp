Appearance

What receives light and dimming

Object lighting follows each object's position, consistently across its sprite parts. Dimming can affect scenery, the player and enemies independently. Unchecked override controls keep inherited behavior.

Position and shape

Offsets use the effect's own layer pixels; map handles account for its parallax. Scale 1 keeps its original size, and direction 0 keeps its original angle. Source art remains required. These edits do not move terrain or gameplay objects. Receiver controls belong to the parent source family.

Attach to an actor or attack

Advanced identity filters

Attached X/Y are offsets from each actor's world origin. Family IDs use the native game's identities across regional art and terrain. Optional state/visual filters separate an attack from its parent. Preview supplies one visual actor sample at the reference actor; it does not run AI or spawn game objects. All matches share this definition and its rendering budget.

Position and area

Drag along the visible rock edge. Up to 16 points per stroke, within 512px. Explicit points let you avoid soft background details. Check tile depth and collision before tracing.

Particle movement and size

Sizes are radii in game pixels. Travel uses pixels per cycle: negative Y rises, positive Y falls, zero Y stays within the area. Spread 0 forms a column; 1 fills the width. Seed fixes the pattern when scrubbing. Changing area size keeps particle size fixed.

Ground mist searches down through the area to the first supported floor in each column. Mist height controls its depth above that floor. Free mist is airborne.
